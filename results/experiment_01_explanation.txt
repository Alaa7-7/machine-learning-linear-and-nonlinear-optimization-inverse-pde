Experiment 01: Simple 1D Heat Equation


1. Idea of the experiment


In this experiment, I used a simple one-dimensional heat equation.

The goal is to simulate how heat moves along a line over time.

I used a simple numerical method to calculate the temperature at different
positions and times.


2. Mathematical equation


The heat equation used in this project is:

u_t = k * u_xx

where:

u = temperature
t = time
x = position
k = heat parameter


3. Numerical equation


To calculate the temperature, I used the following finite difference formula:

u[i] = old_u[i] + k * dt / (dx * dx) * (
    old_u[i + 1] - 2 * old_u[i] + old_u[i - 1]
)


4. Meaning of i


The variable i represents the position along the line.

For example:

i = 1  , first point inside the line
i = 2  , second point
i = 3  , third point

The program calculates the temperature at each point using i.

For each point i, the program uses three temperatures:

old_u[i - 1]  , temperature at the point on the left
old_u[i]      , temperature at the current point
old_u[i + 1]  , temperature at the point on the right

This allows the program to calculate how heat moves from one point
to the next point.


5. Parameters used


k = 0.1

length = 1.0

time = 0.5

nx = 20

nt = 50

dx = length / nx

dt = time / nt


6. Boundary conditions


At the left side, the temperature is fixed at:

u[0] = 100

At the right side, the temperature is fixed at:

u[nx] = 0

This means that the left side is hot and the right side is cold.


7. Result


The program gave:

Heat equation experiment

k = 0.1
Temperature at the middle = 11.487271887444848
Temperature at the right side = 0.0


8. Simple interpretation


The result shows that the temperature at the middle of the line is about:

11.49

The temperature at the right side is:

0

This is expected because the right boundary was fixed at zero temperature.

The experiment shows that the numerical method can simulate the
temperature distribution of a simple 1D heat equation.


9. Conclusion


In this first experiment, I implemented a simple numerical solution
of the 1D heat equation.

I used finite differences to calculate the temperature at different
positions.

The variable i was used to move through the points inside the line.

The result gives a starting point for the next experiment, where I will
solve an inverse problem and try to estimate the unknown parameter k.
